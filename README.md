# Leadership in the Age of AI — A Short Reflection

A self-contained, browser-based questionnaire that helps leaders reflect on how
they lead — not the tools they use — as AI reshapes technical work. It is the
take-home companion to two talks on **authentic** and **inclusive** leadership,
and draws on research into **women's path to technical leadership**.

## What it does

The questionnaire asks eight short questions (plus one optional), then turns the
answers into a personal reflection:

- **A snapshot** — a six-bar chart showing where you stand across dimensions
  such as whether leadership rewards capability over volume, whether the leaders
  you can name reflect the women doing the work, recent sponsorship, and whether
  the quietest voices are heard.
- **A conclusion** — a short, tailored interpretation: an overall reading, the
  area where attention would matter most, a strength to build on, and a tie back
  to your chosen commitment.
- **A commitment** — one concrete leadership habit to carry into your next
  project.

It takes about four minutes.

## How to use it

- **On your own:** open the link, answer honestly, and use *Copy my reflection*
  or *Save as PDF / print* to keep your result.
- **As a session take-home:** share the link or a QR code pointing to it so
  attendees can reflect after a talk.
- **For a live group poll:** this file gives each person a *private* reflection
  and does not pool responses. For aggregated, real-time results during a
  session, recreate the questions in a tool such as Slido or Mentimeter.

## Privacy

The questionnaire runs entirely in the browser. Nothing is transmitted or
collected; answers stay on the visitor's own device. (On a hosted page, saved
answers may not persist across reloads — by design, there is no server.)

## Running it locally

It is a single HTML file with no build step. Open `index.html` in any modern
browser, or serve the folder with any static server.

## A note on method

The reflection snapshot is a structured mirror, not a validated psychometric
instrument. The scoring is reasonable but illustrative, which is why the result
is framed as *what your answers suggest* rather than as a grade. Use it to
prompt honest reflection and conversation.
